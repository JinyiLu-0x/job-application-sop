# Accepted Pattern Case Study

This reference records the design principles accepted during the Suncorp Technology Internship application. It is a pattern, not a fixed letter.

## What worked

1. A direct opening: applying for the named internship in the named city, followed by current degree, major, and graduation timing.
2. A short transition introducing resume highlights.
3. Four concise bullets covering Business Analysis interest, business and technology connection, project-based learning with AI workflows, and initiative in collaborative learning.
4. Supporting paragraphs in the same order as the bullets.
5. EcoSkin as the main process and workflow example.
6. Codex, Obsidian, and a personal website as evidence of active learning and improving effectiveness.
7. Google Developer Groups on Campus and Cisco mentoring as restrained evidence of contribution and seeking opportunities.
8. A company paragraph connecting cloud, AI, and automation to an insurance setting where systems must be effective, safe, and accountable.
9. A brief closing and conventional sign-off.
10. A plain one-page A4 layout with a serif font, left alignment, and no decorative elements.

## What did not work

1. Inventing or assuming a team name.
2. Adding a "Re:" line when the selected template did not include one.
3. Writing long blocks without a quick-scanning bullet section.
4. Using bullets without short paragraphs that prove them.
5. Making the bullets so long that they become paragraphs.
6. Repeating the resume instead of selecting evidence.
7. Calling vague contribution "team communication" when the verified role was visual design.
8. Emphasising being selected for an opportunity rather than the decision to seek learning.
9. Using too many sentences beginning with "I also".
10. Overly simple wording that loses professionalism, or overly polished wording that sounds artificial.
11. Generic praise such as saying that the company "appeals" to the candidate.
12. Rewriting approved content when only a small wording correction was requested.

## Reusable language patterns

These are structural cues, not mandatory sentences:

1. "I am applying for [Company]'s [Program] in [Location]."
2. "Let me summarise some highlights from my resume:"
3. "I tend to learn through projects."
4. "I make a point of learning in collaborative settings."
5. "I look forward to hearing from you and discussing my application further."

Keep or adapt these only when they remain natural in the full letter.
