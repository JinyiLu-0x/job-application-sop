# Cover Letter SOP

## Research before drafting

Confirm the exact role title, company, location, program dates, discipline, employment type, eligibility, and any explicitly named values or technologies. Use current first-party sources where possible. Do not infer a team name from a generic careers page.

Create a short evidence map before writing:

1. Employer need.
2. Candidate evidence.
3. Why the evidence matters in this employer's environment.

## Accepted one-page structure

1. Candidate name and contact details, left aligned.
2. Date.
3. Salutation.
4. Direct opening paragraph naming the role, company, location, current degree, major, and expected graduation date when relevant.
5. A transition such as "Let me summarise some highlights from my resume:".
6. A compact block of three or four bullets.
7. Short paragraphs that support those bullets in the same order with concrete evidence.
8. One company-specific paragraph explaining the fit without generic praise.
9. A concise closing, followed by "Yours sincerely" and the candidate's name.

Use `assets/cover-letter-template.md` as the base structure. The template is not fixed wording. Adapt it to the evidence and employer.

## Bullet block

The bullets should help a recruiter understand the application quickly. Each bullet should be one line where possible and express a distinct theme. Strong themes include:

1. Role direction or domain interest.
2. Connection between business needs, process, and technology.
3. Project-based learning and practical AI workflow use.
4. Initiative in seeking collaborative learning opportunities.

Do not use bullets for vague adjectives or a long skills inventory. The paragraphs below must add evidence, not repeat the bullets word for word.

## Supporting paragraphs

Keep each paragraph focused on one theme. Use one or two strong examples rather than listing every project.

For Business Analysis roles, explain process connections, repeated work, clarity, requirements, constraints, implementation, and human handoffs. Present the software background as a bridge to technical delivery.

For AI and automation, focus on effectiveness, context management, testing, human review, safety, and accountability. In insurance, finance, government, or other regulated environments, connect automation to responsible use without pretending to have industry expertise that has not been earned.

For collaboration, describe the contribution. Do not replace evidence with "team player". For a design role, say what design work or representation was performed rather than claiming vague communication support.

## Company paragraph

Use only verified company facts that connect directly to the candidate's working style or evidence. A good paragraph answers:

1. Why this environment is relevant to the candidate.
2. What the candidate could contribute as an intern.
3. What the candidate is prepared to learn.

Avoid "appeals to me", generic compliments, and a list of corporate values copied from the website.

## Language and format

1. Use concise Australian English.
2. Keep the page to one A4 page unless the employer requests otherwise.
3. Use a conventional 11 or 12 point serif or sans serif font.
4. Keep all text left aligned with consistent paragraph spacing.
5. Do not add a subject or "Re:" line when the chosen template does not use one.
6. Do not add decorative colour, sidebars, graphics, or excessive bolding.
7. Use "Dear [Company] Recruitment Team" when no person is named and the organisation clearly has a recruitment function. Use "Dear Sir/Madam" only when appropriate. Do not assume the reader is a manager.
8. Keep the close brief. Do not add a long sentimental paragraph.

## Revision protocol

After every iteration, show only the changed passage unless the user asks to see the full letter. Lock accepted paragraphs. When the user requests a small word change, do not restructure the letter.
