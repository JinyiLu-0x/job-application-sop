# Run Job Applications

## Purpose

Turn a job advertisement and the candidate's current documents into a truthful, tailored application. Work collaboratively in Chinese during reasoning and iteration, then produce concise professional English when the application requires it.

This workflow is employer-centred. Explain what the employer needs, select the candidate evidence that answers it, and avoid writing a biography or repeating the resume.

## Required Workflow

### 1. Establish the source of truth

1. Read `references/current-materials.md` to locate the current local source files and identify facts that still require confirmation.
2. Collect the complete job advertisement, current resume, current cover letter if one exists, application questions, location preference, graduation date, work-right status, and submission deadline.
3. Treat the newest user-provided document or answer as current. Flag contradictions instead of silently choosing one, and update the material index when the user confirms that a newer file is the new baseline.
4. Verify unstable company, program, salary, deadline, eligibility, and work-right information from authoritative current sources when needed.
5. Never invent a team, stream, recruiter, hiring manager, office, technology, value, or program feature.
6. Do not copy personal identifiers into this Skill. Use them only in the live application when the user supplies or confirms them.

Read `references/user-profile.md` for stable positioning and style preferences. Read `references/quality-gates.md` before accepting any claim as final.

### 2. Build a role evidence map

Extract the following before drafting:

1. Exact role title and company name.
2. Program dates, location, employment type, stream, and eligibility.
3. Three to five capabilities the employer appears to value.
4. Matching evidence from study, projects, work, leadership, or opportunity-seeking.
5. Gaps, risks, and facts requiring confirmation.
6. A recommended positioning angle, such as Business Analysis, software development, AI workflow improvement, or consulting.

Present the recommendation plainly in Chinese. Separate confirmed facts from interpretation.

### 3. Choose the requested task

Use only the references needed for the current step:

1. Opportunity discovery, forgotten search terms, formal online-application queries, LinkedIn Posts queries, or evaluation of post-sourced opportunities: read `references/opportunity-search.md`. Treat formal online applications as the regular route and all opportunities discovered through Posts as the separate non-standard sourcing route. Return to the main workflow after selecting a concrete opportunity.
2. Recruiter email, message, interview answer, or other communication: read `references/communication-sop.md`.
3. Application form: read `references/application-forms.md` and answer one question at a time.
4. Cover letter: read `references/cover-letter-sop.md` and `references/accepted-patterns.md`.
5. Resume review or tailoring: read `references/resume-sop.md`.
6. Final submission: read `references/quality-gates.md` and perform every applicable check.

### 4. Iterate at the requested scale

1. When the user asks for a small word change, change only that word or sentence and show the exact change.
2. Do not rewrite an approved paragraph unless a factual error, serious grammar problem, or explicit user instruction requires it.
3. Keep an approved structure stable while fixing references, transitions, repeated words, or formatting.
4. Explain material changes in Chinese and keep the explanation shorter than the draft.
5. If the user says an answer is accepted, treat it as locked until new evidence conflicts with it.

### 5. Produce the correct output

Follow `references/output-contract.md`. For Word or PDF deliverables, use the relevant document skill, render the file, visually inspect every page, and ensure the final application remains one page when that is the agreed constraint.

## Non-Negotiable Rules

1. Truth before polish. Never upgrade limited work rights to full or unlimited rights.
2. Employer perspective before self-description. Show how evidence meets the role.
3. Specific evidence before adjectives. Replace claims such as "excellent team player" with a verified example.
4. Natural professional English. Avoid robotic transitions, inflated claims, generic company praise, and long abstract sentences.
5. Minimal revision means minimal revision.
6. Use Australian English unless the employer requests another variant.
7. Avoid dash punctuation in prose because the user prefers sentences and commas.
8. Do not expose private data in reusable templates, public examples, repository descriptions, or Skill references.
9. Prepare and review by default. Do not click a final Submit button, send an email or message, sign a declaration, accept legal terms, or transmit an application to an employer or third party unless the user gives explicit authorization for that specific external action in the current task.
10. Permission to research, draft, edit, upload for preview, or fill fields is not permission to submit. Stop at the final review screen, summarise what will be sent, and ask for confirmation when explicit submission authorization is absent.

## Final Handoff

Give the user:

1. The exact recommended answer or final document.
2. Any unresolved factual risk that could affect eligibility or credibility.
3. A short list of changes only when changes were made.
4. The next application question when using the one-question-at-a-time form workflow.
